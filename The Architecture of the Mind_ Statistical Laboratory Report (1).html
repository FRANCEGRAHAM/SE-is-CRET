What is K-means clustering?

K-means is a statistical method that sorts respondents into a chosen number of groups (k) according to how similar their scores are. Here k = 2, and each student is described by three numbers: the mean scores for Part 1, Part 2 and Part 3.

  1. The program places two starting centers (centroids) in the score space.
  2. Each student is assigned to the nearest center.
  3. Each center moves to the average of the students assigned to it.
  4. Steps 2 and 3 repeat until no student changes group.

How to read the result. The group with the higher centroid is labeled Structured "Holmesian" and the other group is labeled Less-structured. Since there are no Chaotic thinkers among the respondents (no student fell in the Very low preference band, 1.00-1.74, and only one fell in the Low preference band, 1.75-2.49), the second cluster is not a Chaotic Profile. Instead, a different cluster was formed: the Less-structured Profile. These students scored lower than the Holmesian cluster but still endorsed structured practices (cluster mean 3.09, Agree). Both groups are relative profiles of this sample, not fixed types. K-means always returns exactly k groups, even when scores form one continuous range, so the groups do not prove that two natural thinking styles exist.

Notes and limitations

  • Three cells in which two answers were ticked were treated as missing, so those items have n = 33 or 34.
  • The researcher's own response (respondent 1 of 36) was excluded, so N = 35.
  • Six respondents gave the same answer to all 30 items (three all SA, three all A), which may inflate reliability and affect the clusters.
  • Scores are self-reported. Low scores indicate lower endorsement of structured practices; they do not establish latent inhibition, divergent thinking or a chaotic cognitive profile.
  • Content validity of the instrument has not been established; expert validation is recommended.
  • The sample is 35 students from one school, so the findings describe this group only.
